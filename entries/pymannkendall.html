Summary

The Yue–Wang modified Mann–Kendall trend test is meant to correct for autocorrelation. In pyMannKendall (yue_wang_modification_test with its default lag=None) and in R's modifiedmk (mmky()), it uses the sample autocorrelation at every lag with no screening. That shrinks the variance instead of correcting it: about 47% of pure-noise series are declared to have a significant trend at the 5% level.

What we found

  • The variance is multiplied by F = 1 + 2Σk=1n−1(1 − k/n) rk, summed over all lags of the Sen-detrended series. For independent noise F averages about 1/3 before detrending and about 0.15 after, where the correct value is about 1.
  • Rejection rates at the nominal 5% level, 10,000 simulations per case:

  • Larger samples do not help, and at p < 0.001 a quarter of white-noise series are still "significant".
  • The Sen slope itself is unaffected; only the variance, p-values and significance labels change.
  • pyMannKendall's lag=1 option keeps only the lag-1 term and behaves far better (6–7%).

Prior work

The problem is not wholly new: O'Brien et al. (2021, Water Resources Research) reported "unacceptably high" type I errors for the all-lags form, and Militino et al. (2020, Remote Sensing) found a mean type I error of 0.49. What is new here is the exact algebraic diagnosis, the defaults in the two most-used packages, and a rerun of a published study.

Why it matters

Both packages are widely used for hydrological and climate trend studies. In a rerun of a published German Bight sea-surface-temperature trend study, the share of grid cells with a significant decline fell from 55% to 23–25% under calibrated tests, and to 14–18% after field-significance control. The main offshore decline holds.

Response

Reported as pyMannKendall #41 and modifiedmk #4 on 6 November 2026. The modifiedmk maintainer asked for a pull request, which adds a lag limit and documents the type I error (PR #5). No pyMannKendall response yet.

What users can do

Use lag=1, an AR(1)-based correction, or a block bootstrap instead of the all-lags default, and recheck significance in published results that used it.
