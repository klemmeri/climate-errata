Site updates

Changes to the Climate Errata website itself: new features, new pages and changes to how it works. Updates to the errata are in the register and its feed.

RSS feed of site updates · Suggestions for the site are welcome through Write to us.

A place on every entry for authors to respond or object

2026-11-24

Every entry now has a short section for the authors, data producers or developers of the work. They can send a response or an objection through Write to us. We review every response promptly; if it shows our finding is wrong, we correct or withdraw the entry openly. A response is shown on the entry, unedited, only if its author wants it there. The Policies page was also updated: an objection does not by itself remove a tested finding, entries never allege misconduct or speculate about motives, and a reported error in an entry is marked as under review until it is resolved.

Boers / Boulton entry relabeled "Reported"

2026-11-24

The entry on the surrogate significance test in Boers (2021) and Boulton et al. (2022) previously had the outcome "Acknowledged", based on private correspondence. Under our policy that replies are described only with permission, it now reads "Reported" (emails to the authors, November 2026). The finding itself is unchanged.

RTE+RRTMGP summary clarified

2026-11-23

The one-line summary of the RTE+RRTMGP entry now says that the 70–80% understatement applies when a key gas is zero or near zero (such as CO2-removal experiments or configurations that switch a gas off), rather than reading as a general understatement of CO2 effects. The finding is unchanged.

Checks for authors: unpublished manuscripts paused

2026-11-23

Because the analysis runs on an AI service in the cloud, and we cannot yet tell authors exactly how files sent there are stored and for how long, <a href="/check.html">Ask for a check</a> now covers published papers and public preprints only. Unpublished and embargoed manuscripts are not accepted for now.

Correction: Hugonnet et al. 2021 rescored Medium; verdicts shown on the register; confidentiality of checks clarified

2026-11-23

The Hugonnet et al. (2021) glacier entry was scored High with Consequence 2. Under our rubric, Consequence 2 requires a published headline to change; here the interval widens but the acceleration remains significant, so Consequence is 1 and the entry is Medium (Size 1, Reach 2, Consequence 1). The register list now shows each entry's verdict (for example, "Real error; claim survives") next to its status. The <a href="/check.html#confidential">Ask for a check</a> page now states that the analysis runs on an AI service in the cloud, so files sent for a check are processed there. The MERRA-2 Australia entry now links its year-by-year totals as a CSV file instead of offering scripts on request. These changes follow an outside review of the site.

Correction: climdex.pcic entry relabeled "Reported"

2026-11-23

The climdex.pcic WSDI/CSDI entry was labeled "Confirmed". The developers have not yet confirmed the finding or merged the proposed fix (pull request #46), so the outcome now reads "Reported". The finding itself is unchanged.

Confidential checks for authors and reproduction statements

2026-11-23

Authors can now ask for a free, confidential check of the statistics and data handling in their own paper, preprint or manuscript. Nothing about a check is posted unless the author asks. When we reproduce a result, we offer the authors a short independent reproduction statement they can cite. See <a href="/check.html">Ask for a check</a>.

Lighter-check label and fewer, informational messages to authors

2026-11-23

"What we checked" now distinguishes papers whose methods we reviewed without rerunning anything ("Method reviewed, not rerun") from those we reproduced or tested. Notes on papers whose authors we have not contacted now describe only what we did and the outcome. Messages to authors are now sent only for findings we have tested ourselves and rate Medium or High; they are for information, ask for no reply, and say that the analysis was AI-assisted and checked by the editor.

Clearer rules on author replies and on what an entry claims

2026-11-23

First messages to authors and producers now say that a finding may be recorded here. Their replies are published, quoted or summarized only with their explicit permission; without it, an entry gives only our own assessment and says that they were contacted. A new section of the Policies page, "What an entry claims", says that every entry is our own assessment, links to its method and code, and that High findings are also written up for a formal, citable outlet.

Significance labels now link to how they are scored

2026-11-22

Every "Significance: High / Medium / Low" label in the register, search results, dataset pages and entry pages now links to the scoring rubric on the About page (size of the effect, reach of the affected paper, dataset or code, and consequence for published results). The register also has a one-line explanation under its filters.

New page: What we checked

2026-11-22

A new page lists every paper, dataset and piece of code we have checked, with the outcome, including the many with no error. Papers (screened systematically since 22 November 2026) are tallied separately for those supporting and those questioning mainstream climate findings. Items whose possible errors are still with the authors, producers or developers are counted but not named until they reply or three weeks pass. The page is in the menu as "What we checked".

What we select for review, and where from

2026-11-22

The How it works page now explains how papers, datasets and code are chosen: most-cited recent papers in five major journals, widely cited papers that question mainstream findings (tested the same way), widely used datasets and open-source climate software. Substack, blogs, news, social media and non-peer-reviewed preprints are out of scope.

Each entry now starts with what the dataset, paper or code is

2026-11-22

Every entry page now opens with a short "What it is" description of the dataset, paper or software concerned, before the finding, and the register shows the same description on each card. Also fixed: on ten entry pages that link to our Zenodo write-ups, the status label at the top wrongly read "Our write-up on Zenodo (latest version)" instead of the entry's status.

Fixes we propose; our reports on Zenodo

2026-11-22

The How it works page now explains the fixes we send with reports: patches and pull requests for software, affected areas and workarounds for datasets, and corrected analyses for papers. It also lists our two broader reports on Zenodo. The home page mentions the proposed fixes too.

Entries link to our Zenodo preprints

2026-11-22

Entries backed by one of our Zenodo preprints now link to it on the entry page. The links use Zenodo's all-versions DOI, so they always open the latest version. Links were added for the modifiedmk, Ali et al. 2026 and Jin & Pu 2026 entries, and the hourly-precipitation entry now opens version 3 rather than version 2.

New address: climateerrata.org

2026-11-22

The site now lives at climateerrata.org. The old address, climate-errata.netlify.app, redirects there automatically, so existing links keep working.

Full write-ups for the High-significance entries

2026-11-22

The 11 entries rated High significance now have full write-ups: what was found, with the key numbers, who is affected, the responsible party's response, what users can do, and how to reproduce the result. The other entries will follow.

Fix details on every fixed entry

2026-11-22

Each fixed entry now shows when the fix was made, which version or pull request contains it, which versions are affected, and whether the fix is retroactive. The register separates fixes that are released from fixes merged but not yet released. Release statuses were rechecked on GitHub and CRAN.

Policies and privacy page

2026-11-22

A new page covers contact first, author responses and disputes, corrections and withdrawals, moderation, a code of conduct, funding and conflicts of interest, use of AI, privacy, and takedown or legal requests. It is linked from the top menu and every page footer.

Papers that use the affected data

2026-11-22

Entries about a problem in a dataset can now list papers that use the affected data for the affected region and period. The effect on each paper is not assessed. The first lists are on the MERRA-2 Australia and ASOS wind entries.

Dataset pages

2026-11-22

A new Datasets section has one page for each of 14 widely used datasets (ERA5, MERRA-2, CPC Unified, GPCC, GPCP, IMERG, E-OBS, ICOADS, HadSST4, OISST, IBTrACS, COOP HPD, ASOS, GHCN-CAMS). Each page gives the current version, the producer's known-issues page and contact, our entries, related discussions, a place to ask questions, and an RSS feed. There is also a feed for the whole register.

Discussion by DOI, site search and a contact form

2026-11-22

Any paper or dataset can now have a discussion page: enter its DOI on Discuss a DOI. Search covers the register, the datasets and the discussions. Write to us takes questions, private error reports and author responses from people without a GitHub account. A posting policy asks that new error claims go to the responsible party first.

Climate Errata launched

2026-11-22

The register of errors found in climate papers, datasets and code moved from its earlier home to this site, with 48 entries, filters, a significance rubric and a comment thread on every entry.
