How Climate Errata works

An independent register of errors and issues in climate science: published papers, gridded and station datasets, and the software used to analyse them.

What we check

Methods, statistics, data processing and code. Most entries come from reproducing a result with the published data and code, or from comparing a dataset with independent references and station records.

Contact first

Every finding is sent privately to the authors, data producers or developers before anything is posted. An entry appears here only after they reply or three weeks pass without a reply. Their response is recorded on the entry.

Statuses

  • Reported sent to the responsible party; no resolution yet.
  • Acknowledged the responsible party agrees there is an issue.
  • Disputed the responsible party disagrees; both views are shown.
  • Fixed corrected in a new version, erratum or release.
  • No error found checked; the claims or code hold.
  • Already known the issue was already documented elsewhere.

How the work is done

The analyses were carried out with an AI system (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction. The author checked the methods and results and is responsible for the content.

Significance

Each finding that claims a problem is scored 0–2 on three criteria. The class comes from the total (0–6): High 5–6, Medium 3–4, Low 0–2. Clean checks and already-known issues are not scored.

Reliability of the scoring

Independent raters applied the written rubric blind to all 80 findings in the full audit (public and not yet public). Agreement with the first rater (weighted Cohen's kappa), rubric v1 → v2: Size 0.77 → 0.81; Reach 0.68 → 0.73; Consequence 0.62 → 0.73; Class 0.62 (65% exact) → 0.70 (79% exact). Between rounds one rule was clarified: a developer-confirmed or merged fix alone counts as C = 1, not C = 2. All raters so far are AI instances; a human rater on a random sample is planned. Reach scores marked "est." are estimates to be replaced by measured download or citation counts.

Rules

  • We discuss methods and data, never people or motives.
  • Every claim is backed by evidence that can be rerun.
  • If we get something wrong, we correct the entry openly and date the change.

Discussion and questions

Climate Errata is also a place for researchers to ask about the datasets and papers they use. Every register entry has a discussion thread. Any paper or dataset can have one too: enter its DOI on the Discuss a DOI page, and everyone asking about the same work shares one thread. Ask other readers, the authors or the maintainer. Search covers both the register and the discussions.

Threads use GitHub Discussions, so commenting needs a free GitHub account. Without one, use Write to us.

Posting policy

  • Questions, answers and discussion are welcome on any thread: how a dataset was built, what changed between versions, whether a method suits a purpose, how to reproduce a result.
  • New error claims go to the responsible party first. If you think you have found an error that has not been reported, tell the authors or data producers, or send it to us privately. We check it, contact them and give them at least three weeks, the same rule we follow ourselves.
  • Comments that make a new, unreported error claim about someone's work may be hidden until the responsible party has been contacted. They are restored once that has happened. Claims about issues already public (a published erratum, a known-issues page, an open GitHub issue) are fine; please link the source.
  • Discuss methods and data, never people or motives. Be specific and give the version, region and period you looked at.
  • Authors and data producers are invited to reply on any thread, or to send a response for the entry itself through Write to us.
  • Off-topic, abusive or promotional comments are removed. Moderation decisions are made by the maintainer.
