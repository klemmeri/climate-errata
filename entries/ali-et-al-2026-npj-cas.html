Ali et al. 2026, npj CAS (hurricane rainfall scaling)

Public commentpaperSignificance: High

What it is

Ali et al. (2026, npj Climate and Atmospheric Science) estimated how hurricane rainfall increases with sea-surface temperature, reporting about 21% more rain per degree of warming.

Finding

Headline 21%/K not reproduced (12%); within-storm ~2%/K

VerdictReal error (not reproducible)

OutcomePublic comment

Summary

Ali et al. (2026, npj Climate and Atmospheric Science) reported that North Atlantic tropical-cyclone rainfall intensity rises about 21% per degree of local dewpoint, three times the Clausius–Clapeyron rate, and a press release linked this to warming. Rebuilding their method for 2015–2024, we get 12.3% per K, well below their value. Within individual storms, after controlling for latitude, intensity and speed, the scaling is about 2% per K. We found no sign that warmer-than-usual seas give wetter storms.

What we found

172 storms, 3,006 six-hourly steps, 10 seasons (IMERG V07, ERA5, IBTrACS). 95% intervals from a storm bootstrap:

Test%/K
Paper's method, dewpoint under the heavy rain (paper: 21.3)12.3 [9.2, 15.4]
Same with 2-m temperature (paper: 19.6) / SST (paper: 15.7)10.1 [7.6, 13.3] / 10.2 [7.8, 12.6]
Dewpoint in the storm's environment (500–800 km)9.0 [6.0, 11.3]
Pooled regression with latitude, intensity and speed controls7.3 [4.3, 10.5]
Within storms, with controls1.7 [−2.1, 5.3]
Warmer-than-usual SST for place and season0.7 [−7.6, 9.9]
Trend in storm rain intensity0.9 %/yr [−1.0, 2.7]
  1. The headline number did not reproduce. Differences in method details (the storm-radius definition, sampling, and our shorter period) may explain part of the gap; the authors' code is not public, and we asked for the radius definition.
  2. The super-Clausius–Clapeyron scaling is a between-storm effect. It disappears within storms once intensity and location are controlled.
  3. Where temperature is measured matters: dewpoint under the heavy rain gives much higher scaling than the environmental dewpoint.
  4. No temporal signal: the paper's scaling is cross-sectional ("apparent"), as the authors note; the data show no evidence that warming over time has made these storms wetter.

Response

The lead author was emailed on 11 November 2026 with the preprint and a question about the storm-radius definition. PubPeer comment posted 13 November 2026. No reply yet.
