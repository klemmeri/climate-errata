MetPy vorticity/divergence

Reported (open)codeSignificance: High

What it is

MetPy is a widely used Python library for meteorological calculations. Its vorticity and divergence functions compute rotation and spreading of the wind from gridded data.

Finding

Map-factor error grows toward the poles (~10,000× at pole rows)

VerdictReal error
SignificanceHigh (Size 2 · Reach 2 · Consequence 1 = 5 of 6)
Basis: Error up to ~10,000x at pole rows (S, local); MetPy dominant meteorology library (R); no rerun (C)
OutcomeOpen
Where reportedGitHub #4208
https://github.com/Unidata/MetPy/issues/4208
Date2026-11-12

Summary

MetPy, the standard Python meteorology library, computes vorticity, divergence and related quantities on latitude–longitude grids with a map-factor correction whose derivative is taken by differencing across grid points. Near the poles that difference is badly wrong. The error grows from a few percent at 80° to hundreds of percent within a degree or two of the pole. On grids that include the pole rows (ERA5, GFS), the pole row and its neighbour return finite values about 104 times the true field, not NaN.

What we found

Test case: an n = 2 spherical-harmonic streamfunction whose vorticity is known exactly. Maximum error as a percentage of the field maximum (MetPy 1.7.1):

Grid70°80°85°Near the pole
2.5°1.2%12%125%—
1°0.19%1.8%16%318% at 88°
0.25°0.01%0.11%0.94%1,270% at 89.5°
  • A standard second-order scheme with the exact tanφ/a term gives at most 0.3% at all these points.
  • Cause: vector_derivative differentiates the parallel scale 1/cos φ across grid points; the derivatives of u and v themselves are fine.
  • Affected: vorticity, absolute_vorticity, divergence, the deformation functions, shear_vorticity, curvature_vorticity, q_vector, geospatial_laplacian (79% at 87° on a 1° grid) and potential vorticity through vorticity. Scalar advection and gradients are not affected.
  • MetPy's own tests compare against the same discrete approach, so they cannot catch it. A user on the Pangeo forum had reported an "explosion of derivatives near the North Pole" without finding the cause.

Who is affected

Polar work: Arctic cyclones, polar vortex and stratospheric warming studies, Antarctic analyses, and CMIP or reanalysis output on 1–2.5° grids. Pole-row spikes silently distort colour scales, zonal means and spectra. Equatorward of about 70° on fine grids the effect is small.

Suggested fix

Take the map-factor derivatives from the projection itself with a tiny offset (pyproj get_factors at ±10−4°) instead of differencing across grid points, and return NaN on pole rows. In a prototype the errors fall 15 to 50,000 times: 125% → 0.23% at 85° on a 2.5° grid, and 1,270% → 0.02% at 89.5° on a 0.25° grid.

Response

Reported as MetPy #4208 on 12 November 2026 with the test, the prototype fix and an offer of a pull request. Open.

What users can do

Mask results poleward of about 80° (85° on fine grids), drop the pole rows, or compute polar kinematics with a spherical-harmonic method.
