How Climate Errata works

An independent register of errors and issues in climate science: published papers, gridded and station datasets, and the software used to analyse them.

What we check

Methods, statistics, data processing and code. Most entries come from reproducing a result with the published data and code, or from comparing a dataset with independent references and station records.

Contact first

Every finding is sent privately to the authors, data producers or developers before anything is posted. An entry appears here only after they reply or three weeks pass without a reply. Their response is recorded on the entry.

Statuses

  • Reported sent to the responsible party; no resolution yet.
  • Acknowledged the responsible party agrees there is an issue.
  • Disputed the responsible party disagrees; both views are shown.
  • Fixed corrected in a new version, erratum or release.

Significance

Each entry is scored on three things, each 0–2: the size of the effect on results (S), the reach of the paper, dataset or code (R), and whether the conclusions or a released product must change (C). Total 5–6 is High, 3–4 Medium, 0–2 Low.

Rules

  • We discuss methods and data, never people or motives.
  • Every claim is backed by evidence that can be rerun.
  • If we get something wrong, we correct the entry openly and date the change.

Comments

Each entry has a discussion thread powered by GitHub Discussions. Anyone with a GitHub account can comment; authors and developers are especially welcome.
