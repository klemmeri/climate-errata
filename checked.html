What we checked

Every paper, dataset and piece of code we have checked, with the outcome, whether or not we found an error. What we choose to check, and why, is explained on the How it works page.

When we find a possible error, the authors, data producers or developers hear about it first. Until they reply or three weeks pass, such items are counted here but not named; then they are added by name with a link to their register entry. Papers · Datasets · Code

Mainstream findings

  • Henley et al. 2024, Nature: Coral Sea ocean heat highest in four centuries (Great Barrier Reef). No error found. Reproduced from the authors' code and figure data, including the bound on the pre-1900 maximum.
  • Cai et al. 2024, Nature Geoscience: Summer warming on the Mongolian Plateau, 1986–2004, boosted by internal variability. No error found. Warming rate (about 3.6 times the Northern Hemisphere land average) reproduced in two CRUTEM5 versions; unchanged with unadjusted station data and a stable station network.
  • Wang et al. 2025, Science: Canadian forests more conducive to high-severity fires in recent decades. No material error found. Trend tests and false-discovery-rate control checked; the ERA5 fire-weather inputs were not recomputed.
  • Haslinger et al. 2025, Nature: Increasing hourly heavy rainfall in Austria and flood changes. No error found. Tested for rain-gauge sensor changes with the authors' station data; the increase stands.
  • Bevacqua et al. 2025, Nature Climate Change: A year above 1.5 °C and the Paris Agreement limit. No error found. Idealized experiment reproduced, including with year-to-year persistence.
  • Byrne et al. 2024, Nature: Carbon emissions from the 2023 Canadian wildfires. No error found. Warmest/driest and fossil-emission comparisons checked against independent datasets.
  • Bevacqua et al. 2024, Nature Geoscience: Direct and lagged effects of the 2022 European drought. No error found. Design reviewed; no testable error.
  • Vicente-Serrano et al. 2025, Nature: Mediterranean precipitation: high variability, not trend. No error found. Regional trends, significance tests and the effect of homogenization reproduced from the authors' data.
  • Wang et al. 2024, Science: Anthropogenic influence on river-flow seasonality. No material error found. Alaska stations agree with the paper.
  • Gottlieb & Mankin 2024, Nature: Human influence on Northern Hemisphere snow loss. No material error found
  • Kannenberg et al. 2024, Nature Geoscience: Soil moisture and dryland carbon and water fluxes. No material error found. Method reviewed; flux data not rerun (login required).
  • Esper, Torbenson & Büntgen 2024, Nature: 2023 summer warmth unparalleled over 2,000 years. Already known, corrected or challenged in print. A slip in Table 1 had already been corrected by the authors (May 2025).
  • Zhou, Leung & Lu 2024, Nature Geoscience: Steady threefold Arctic amplification. Already known, corrected or challenged in print. Already challenged in a published comment, with the authors' reply.

Datasets

Observing and gridded datasets checked since our audits began in early November 2026. These checks were not a random sample: we went looking where errors are most likely, such as instrument changes and sudden changes in the number of reporting stations. The share with errors therefore says nothing about how often datasets in general contain errors.

Datasets: no error, no material error, or already known

  • ASOS dew-point sensor swap (DTS1, 2002–06): US airport humidity records. No error found. Transient drift that reset; no lasting step.
  • TAO/TRITON tropical Pacific buoys: Sea-surface temperature and winds. No error found. Clean in the real-time era; the drop in coverage is documented by PMEL.
  • NOAA OISST v2.1 vs TAO buoys: Sea-surface temperature around 2007. No material error found. A shift of about 0.05 °C, below our threshold; part of it is on the buoy side.
  • NOAA OISST v2.1: Recent warming that inflates marine-heatwave trends. Already known, corrected or challenged in print. Already known and discussed in the literature.
  • CRUTEM5: China station temperatures, 2020. Already known, corrected or challenged in print. A step in an older version (5.0.2.0) is already fixed in the current one.
  • E-OBS: Few stations over Algeria and Tunisia. Already known, corrected or challenged in print. A known limitation, documented by the producers.

Code

Climate software checked since our audits began in early November 2026. A line may cover several related packages checked together. These checks were not a random sample either: we chose the most widely used packages and the parts of them most likely to go wrong, such as statistical defaults, edge cases and recently changed code. The share with errors therefore says nothing about how often climate software in general contains errors.
