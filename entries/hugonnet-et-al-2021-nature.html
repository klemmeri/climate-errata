Hugonnet et al. 2021, Nature (accelerated global glacier mass loss, 2000–2019)

ConfirmedpaperSignificance: Medium

What it is

Hugonnet et al. (2021, Nature) mapped thinning of nearly all of the world's ~220,000 glaciers from satellite elevation data and reported that glacier mass loss accelerated over 2000–2019.

Finding

The 95% interval on the mass-loss acceleration (48 ± 16 Gt/yr per decade) was formed by doubling a 1σ interval from a regression with ~2 degrees of freedom; the correct t-based 95% half-width is ~1.64× larger (about ±26). The acceleration remains significant at the corrected level; the author notes conservative assumptions elsewhere (temporal decorrelation, density conversion) likely offset the narrower interval.

Summary

Hugonnet et al. (2021, Nature 592:726) found that global glacier mass loss accelerated over 2000–2019, by 48 ± 16 Gt per year per decade. The ± was meant to be a 95% interval but was formed by doubling a 68% interval from a regression with only two degrees of freedom. The correct 95% interval is about 1.64 times wider, 48 ± 26. The acceleration is still significant. The author confirmed the issue.

What we found

  • The acceleration is a weighted least-squares slope through the four five-year global rates (2000–04 to 2015–19).
  • The fitting routine (wls_matrix in the authors' pyddem package) returns a 68% interval by default: the slope's standard error times t0.84 with n − 2 = 2 degrees of freedom (t = 1.31). The analysis script doubles it and reports it as the 95% value, giving 2.62 standard errors.
  • A 95% t-interval with 2 degrees of freedom is 4.30 standard errors, so the published interval is too narrow by a factor of 1.64.

What it does not change

The acceleration remains significant at the corrected 95% level. The author notes that conservative assumptions elsewhere in the analysis (temporal decorrelation, density conversion) likely offset the narrower interval.

Response

We wrote to the lead author on 19 November 2026. He confirmed that the published interval is understated and will address it in the forthcoming update of the dataset. This entry is listed with his agreement.

Reproduce

From the paper's Table 1 (rates −227 ± 25, −257 ± 22, −284 ± 23, −292 ± 24 Gt/yr) the published recipe gives about ±20 and a proper 95% interval about ±32, the same factor of 1.64. Code: rhugonnet/ww_tvol_study (tables/ms_acce_slr_and_values.py) and pyddem (fit_tools.wls_matrix).
